# 👥 HR Analytics Dashboard

## 📌 Project Overview

Developed an interactive HR Analytics dashboard to analyze employee attrition, workforce demographics, job satisfaction, and departmental turnover patterns.

The project focuses on identifying workforce risks, understanding employee behavior trends, and uncovering the key factors contributing to attrition across departments and demographic groups.

The dashboard transforms HR data into actionable insights that support workforce planning, employee retention, and organizational decision-making.

## 🎯 Business Problem / Objective

Employee attrition can significantly impact productivity, operational continuity, recruitment costs, and long-term organizational growth.

The organization needed visibility into:

- Which departments experience the highest attrition?
- Which employee groups are most likely to leave?
- How does attrition vary across age groups and gender?
- Which education backgrounds show higher turnover?
- What roles show lower job satisfaction?
- Which workforce segments require immediate retention strategies?

This dashboard was designed to help HR leadership proactively identify workforce risks and improve employee retention strategies.

## 🔍 Key Insights

### 📉 R&D Department Faces Critical Attrition Risk

- The R&D department contributes over **56%** of total attrition, making it the primary driver of workforce turnover :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}
- This level of attrition can severely impact:
  - Innovation continuity
  - Product development timelines
  - Institutional knowledge retention
- Persistent turnover in knowledge-intensive roles often indicates deeper issues related to workload, growth opportunities, or organizational culture

### 💼 Sales Department Shows High Workforce Instability

- Sales contributes nearly **39%** of total attrition, making it the second-largest attrition contributor :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}
- High turnover in sales roles can increase:
  - Recruitment costs
  - Revenue instability
  - Client relationship disruption
- The pattern suggests performance pressure and burnout may be contributing factors

### 👨 Young Professionals Represent the Highest Flight Risk

- Employees aged **25–34** show the highest attrition levels, with 112 employees leaving the organization :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}
- This trend is commonly associated with:
  - Career-growth expectations
  - Salary competition
  - Skill mobility in early careers
- The organization may be struggling to retain mid-level talent during critical career-building years

### 👩‍🔬 Employees from Life Sciences & Medical Backgrounds Leave More Frequently

- Employees with Life Sciences and Medical education backgrounds show the highest attrition counts :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}
- This suggests:
  - Higher market demand for these skill sets
  - Greater external opportunities
  - Possible compensation competitiveness issues

### 👨 Male Attrition Significantly Exceeds Female Attrition

- Male employees account for a much larger share of attrition compared to female employees
- This imbalance may indicate:
  - Department-specific workforce concentration
  - Job-role pressure differences
  - Cultural or work-life balance factors

### 😟 Job Satisfaction Issues Exist in Key Operational Roles

- Sales Executives and Laboratory Technicians show large clusters of low satisfaction ratings despite having high employee counts :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}
- This creates a major long-term retention risk because:
  - Low satisfaction often precedes resignation
  - Operational performance may decline
  - Employee engagement weakens over time

### 👴 Senior Employees Show Strong Stability

- Employees above age 55 demonstrate the lowest attrition rates
- Indicates:
  - Greater organizational loyalty
  - Higher role stability
  - Reduced external mobility compared to younger workforce groups

## 💡 Strategic Recommendations

### 🎯 Prioritize Retention in R&D

- Conduct stay interviews and internal surveys within the R&D department
- Investigate workload, career growth, leadership support, and compensation concerns
- Introduce retention-focused career development programs

### 🚀 Strengthen Early-Career Employee Engagement

- Build mentorship and career-pathing programs for employees aged 25–34
- Provide clearer growth opportunities and internal mobility pathways
- Focus on skill development and leadership grooming initiatives

### 💼 Improve Satisfaction in High-Risk Roles

Investigate dissatisfaction drivers among:

- Sales Executives
- Laboratory Technicians

Focus areas should include:

- Workload balance
- Incentive structures
- Career advancement
- Recognition systems

### 📊 Build Proactive Attrition Monitoring

- Develop predictive attrition monitoring systems
- Use HR analytics to identify high-risk employee groups before resignation occurs
- Track satisfaction and engagement trends continuously

### 🤝 Improve Workforce Stability Through Culture & Flexibility

- Strengthen work-life balance initiatives
- Encourage manager-level engagement
- Improve organizational communication and employee recognition
